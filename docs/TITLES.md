# TITLES.md — Title shortlist and name screening

Phase 1.2. Researched 2026-09-16. Decision recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

**Recommendation: keep "Fuse a Nomling".** It survives every check below, and the reason is the noun, not the verb — "Nomling" appears to be unused on Roblox and unclaimed as a trademark, which is exactly what Roblox's discovery rules reward.

---

## What the checks were

1. **Roblox search check** — web search for existing Roblox games using the name or a near-variant. *Caveat: this is an external-search proxy. Roblox's own search needs a logged-in session, so Philip should re-check inside the Roblox app before we commit — it takes 30 seconds and is task 6 in `docs/PHILIP-TODO.md`.*
2. **Trademark-risk note** — web search for existing brands. *Caveat: not a legal clearance search. For a game that might carry real revenue, a proper EUIPO/USPTO search before M5 is cheap insurance.*
3. **Discovery fit** — against the rule Phase 0 verified: games whose "metadata and place files closely resemble existing games" are deprioritized in recommendations and rank lower in search.

## The shortlist

### 1. Fuse a Nomling ✅ recommended

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Roblox search | **No game found** using "Nomling" or "Nomlings". The "nom" space on Roblox is eat-and-grow games ("Nom Your Friends!", "nom nom") — a different genre that does not compete for the name. |
| Trademark | **No brand found.** "Nomling" returns no company, product or mark. Closest unrelated hit was "Nomis", a sports apparel brand — different word, different class. |
| Discovery fit | **Good.** Matches the "Verb a Noun" pattern of current breakouts while the noun is unique, so it reads as part of the trend without duplicating anyone's metadata. |
| Risk | The verb "Fuse" points at the part of the game that is *least* differentiated (see the competitive note below). |

**Why it still wins:** the non-unique-metadata penalty attaches to names and visuals that duplicate an existing game, not to a shared grammatical pattern — there are dozens of "X a Y" titles. "Nomling" is the part that has to be ours, and it is.

### 2. Make a Nomling — the strategic alternative

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Roblox search | Clear (same "Nomling" result). |
| Trademark | Clear. |
| Discovery fit | Same pattern advantage. |
| Risk | "Make" is vaguer than "Fuse" and may read as a dress-up or avatar-creator game, which would attract the wrong players and hurt bounce rate. |

**Worth keeping in view** because it claims what is actually novel here — that you *create a creature nobody has seen* — rather than the fusion mechanic, which the market leader already has. If playtests show people expect the Steal an Egg fusion (combine duplicates → higher tier) and are confused by ours, this title fixes the expectation at the door.

## ⚠️ The competitive finding that matters more than the title

The brief (§4.1, "Genre fit") says the game "combines two of the fastest-growing Roblox formats... with a fusion twist that **no current hit owns**."

**That is not correct as of today.** Research on 2026-09-16 found:

> **Steal An Egg is currently the most-played game on Roblox — roughly 1.7 million concurrent players.**
> Its loop: steal eggs from other players, hatch eggs into pets, pets earn money per second, upgrade your base, and a **Fuse Machine** that combines pets — plus **seven mutations** that multiply income (Rainbow ×3.5, Parasite, Fractured, Golden, Spirit Bloom, Bloom, Silver).

That is our brief's feature list, held by the #1 game on the platform. Fusion is also long-established elsewhere: Pet Simulator X and Pet Simulator 99 both have fuse machines, and Merge Pets!, Pet Merge Simulator, Creatures Tycoon and Collect All Pets all merge pets. Even procedural pets are not virgin ground — **Random Pets World** advertises randomly generated pets with "over a billion combos" and DNA combination.

**What this does and does not mean.**

It does *not* mean the project is dead — a proven, enormous audience for exactly this loop is a real asset, and "too crowded to enter" is not how Roblox works. It *does* mean the differentiation cannot be "we have fusion", because the market leader has fusion and 1.7M players.

**What is actually still ours:**

| | Steal An Egg | Fuse a Nomling |
|---|---|---|
| Creature roster | 157 hand-made pets across 12 biomes | **Thousands, generated from a genome** |
| What fusion produces | A higher tier of an existing pet (3 duplicates in) | **A creature that has never existed, with a generated name** |
| Discovery | Read the index, know every pet | **World First — be the first player anywhere to make one** |

So the defensible pitch is **"the creatures are generated, not listed"** — and the emotional hook is the brief's own first design pillar, *"What did I just make?!"*. Fusion is the mechanism; novelty is the product.

**Consequences carried into the rest of Phase 1:**
- `GDD.md` leads on generation and World First, and treats eggs/stealing as table stakes we must match, not as selling points.
- `MARKETING.md` and `STORE-PAGE.md` sell the reveal of a never-before-seen creature, never "fuse your pets".
- This is risk #1 in the Phase 1.4 challenge list.
- `docs/PHILIP-TODO.md` task 6 (title check) stays open until Philip confirms in Roblox's own search.

## Base Nomling name screening

Screened for brands and trademarks per brief §4.1. Verdicts:

| Name | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Sushi Pup, Pizza Penguin, Donut Duck, Burger Bear, Noodle Frog, Popcorn Pig, Waffle Walrus, Cupcake Croc, Mochi Mole, Nacho Narwhal | ✅ Generic food + generic animal. No brand risk. Descriptive combinations like these are not protectable by anyone, including us. |
| **Taco Cat** | ⚠️ **Replace.** Not a trademark problem in itself, but "Tacocat" is a widely-known palindrome meme and the name of an existing indie band and several published games. It is the one name on the list that someone else has already made famous, which is the opposite of the unique-metadata signal we need. → **Replace with "Taco Tiger"** (alliterative, same joke, clean). |
| **Pickle Parrot** | ⚠️ **Watch.** Clean as a phrase, but "pickle" + a character name sits close to well-known animated-character branding. Low risk; keep for now, revisit if the art leans in a cartoon-mascot direction. |

**Rule for all generated names:** the fusion name generator can emit thousands of strings, so brand screening cannot be manual. `tests/unit` gets a name-safety test (brief §7.6) that enumerates every reachable name and fails CI on any blocklist hit. The blocklist covers profanity in multiple languages **and** a brand list. Detail in `docs/GDD.md`.
